# the404labs content guide

How the404labs, 404shop and 404games talk. It covers every word a visitor reads: headlines, buttons, product blurbs, error messages, FAQs, legal pages, page titles and social cards.

[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) says how things look. This says how they sound. Names and capitalisation are in DESIGN.md under "Name" and are not repeated here.

## The idea

We sound like one person from a small studio talking to one visitor. Friendly, plain, a bit playful, and honest about what's finished and what isn't.

The reference is [The Funday Press](https://thefundaypress.com/). Read its home page and its FAQ before writing anything long. What we take from it:

- **It says what the thing is in one line.** "A monthly newspaper filled with games & fun facts. No news." You know what you're getting before you scroll.
- **Its headings are the visitor's questions.** "What's inside?" "Who's behind this?" Not "Features" and "About".
- **It counts.** "20 handcrafted games and puzzles." A number you can check beats an adjective you can't.
- **It answers first.** Every FAQ answer opens with the answer: "Yes.", "At this time, we're only available within the United States."
- **It sounds like the people who made it.** "If it gets a 'I wanna play again,' it stays." There's a note from the creator, signed.
- **It's warm when something goes wrong.** "Sometimes USPS delivery can take a little longer than expected (ugh!), but we'll do our best to make it right."

## The test

Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it to someone across a table, rewrite it.

Then ask three things:

1. **Is it true today?** Not after the next release. Today.
2. **Could a visitor repeat it?** If they can't say back what the page is for, the first line isn't plain enough.
3. **Would it still be true on a competitor's site?** "Quality products, made with care" fits everyone, so it says nothing. "220gsm cotton, boxy fit" only fits the tee.

## Seven rules

### 1. Say what it is, first

The first line on a page or a section names the thing. Cleverness can come second.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome to a new way to play | Old-school games you can play right in your browser. |
| Elevate your wardrobe | Tees, hoodies and caps from the404labs. |

### 2. Use the words people use

Short, common words. "Use", not "utilise". "Help", not "empower". "Buy", not "purchase". If a term needs explaining, use the explanation and drop the term.

We say "we" and "you". We don't say "users", "customers" or "players" to the person reading; they are "you".

Contractions are fine and usually better: "we're", "it's", "doesn't".

### 3. Be specific

Give the number, the name or the material. Then keep it current: a number that has gone stale is worse than no number.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Premium heavyweight fabric | 400gsm brushed fleece |
| We respond quickly | A real person reads it |
| Lots of fun games | Football, ping pong, running, basketball and bike |

### 4. Tell the truth about where things stand

This is the rule that matters most, because the sites are young and some parts are not finished.

- If checkout is a demo, the page says so, next to the button, in plain words.
- Don't promise what the site can't do. No "we'll email you" unless something actually sends that email.
- Don't promise a feeling. "You'll love it" and "You'll feel calmer" are guesses. "Most people finish a round in a better mood" is a claim, and it needs its source beside it.
- No invented numbers, testimonials, clients or awards. If we don't have one, we don't mention one.
- Placeholder text never ships. If the real words aren't ready, leave the section out.

### 5. One thought per sentence

Short sentences. Most paragraphs are one to three of them. When a sentence wants a dash or a semicolon, it usually wants to be two sentences.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Checkout isn't wired up yet, so nothing ships today — build your cart and we'll let you know. | Checkout isn't wired up yet, so nothing ships today. |
| Not yet — shown prices are the item price. | Not yet. The price shown is the price of the item. |

### 6. One joke per screen, at most

The name is already a joke. The shop's product names are jokes. That is plenty. A joke goes in a headline or a blurb, never in a button, an error message, a price or anything legal. If a joke makes the line less clear, cut the joke.

### 7. Write for the moment the reader is in

Someone who just lost a game wants one line and a way to play again. Someone whose form failed wants to know what happened and what to do. Someone reading the privacy page wants the facts in order. Same voice, different amount of it.

| Moment | How much voice |
| --- | --- |
| Home pages, product blurbs, 404 pages | The most. This is where a joke can go. |
| Forms, settings, checkout, game controls | Almost none. Clear and short. |
| Errors, privacy, terms | None. Plain, calm and complete. |

## How each kind of text works

### Headlines

- Sentence case, no full stop: "Your cart is empty".
- A question is often the best heading, when it's the one the visitor has: "Can I actually buy anything right now?"
- Section labels (eyebrows) are one to three words that say who or what the section is for.

### Body copy

- Lead with the point. Detail comes after, for whoever wants it.
- "We" for the studio, "you" for the reader. Never "the user".
- No sentence should need reading twice.

### Buttons and links

- A button says what happens when you press it: "Add to cart", "Say hello", "Play", "Back to the shop". Two or three words.
- Never "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" or "OK".
- While something is happening, the same verb in progress: "Sending…", "Joining…".
- A link's text makes sense on its own. "Read the privacy page", not "here".

### Empty and loading states

Say what's missing and what to do about it.

- "Your cart is empty. Add something from the shop and it'll show up here."
- "No scores yet"
- "Not in a channel yet. Join one below, or start your own."

### Errors

What happened, then what to do. No blame, no "Oops", no exclamation marks, no error codes on their own.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Oops! Something went wrong. | Your message didn't send. Try again, or email hello@the404labs.co. |
| Invalid input | Channel codes need to be 2 to 24 characters (letters, numbers, spaces, - or _). |
| Error: clipboard unavailable | Couldn't copy automatically. Select the code above and copy it. |

### Confirmations

Say what just happened, in the past tense, and only what's true. A demo checkout doesn't say "Payment successful"; it says the demo is complete and that nothing was charged.

### FAQs

- The question is written the way a visitor would ask it, in the first person: "How do I get in touch?"
- The answer starts with the answer. "Yes." "Not yet." "Email hello@the404labs.co."
- Then one or two sentences of detail. If it needs more, it needs its own page.
- Only real questions. Don't invent one to make room for a sales line.

### Product blurbs (404shop)

One line with the joke or the feeling, then the facts as a list: weight and fabric, fit, how it's printed. Care instructions are instructions and stay dry.

### Game text (404games)

- A game's description is its instruction, in a few words: "Tap the ball to keep it off the ground."
- In-game messages can be lowercase and silly. That's the one place the voice gets loud.
- Anything about a score, a name or a channel is plain: it's the player's data.

### Privacy, terms and anything legal

- Start with the short version in bold, then say "Everything below is all of it" only if it is.
- Name the things: which cookie, which service, how long.
- No jokes. Warmth is fine; it comes from being clear.
- When the code changes what a site stores or sends, the page changes in the same commit.

### Page titles and descriptions

- Title: `404shop • tech merch from the404labs`. Sub-brand, a bullet, a plain description. Sub-pages: `the404labs • privacy`.
- Description: one or two sentences that say what the site is, written to be read on its own in a search result or a link preview. No "Welcome to", no "Check out".
- Social cards use the same line. The words live in [og/cards.json](og/cards.json).

### Accessibility labels

An `aria-label` or alt text is copy too. It says what the control does ("Close cart", "Copy channel code"), not what it looks like ("X button"). When a control's state changes, the label changes with it ("Channel code copied").

## Punctuation and small things

- **No em dashes or en dashes in anything a visitor reads.** Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. A dash usually means two sentences are pretending to be one.
- No exclamation marks in interface text. One in a thank-you message is fine.
- "and", not "&", in sentences. "&" is fine in a tight button label ("Add & view cart").
- The serial comma is optional; be consistent inside one page.
- Numbers as digits: "5 games", "3 sites", "$34".
- The ellipsis is one character (…) and only means "in progress".
- Emoji: none in interface text. In-game banners may use one.
- Sentence case everywhere. Uppercase is a style applied in CSS, never typed.
- Spelling is American in visitor-facing copy (color, canceled), because the studio is in Austin and the shop ships to the United States. Code comments and these documents are not copy.
- Write the email address in full and make it a link: hello@the404labs.co.

## Words

| We say | We don't say |
| --- | --- |
| free to use | free forever, 100% free, no strings attached |
| small | lightweight, bite-sized, micro |
| demo | beta, coming soon, early access (unless it is) |
| a real person | our team, our support staff |
| say hello, email us | reach out, get in touch with our team, contact support |
| build, make | craft, engineer, architect |
| app, site, game | solution, platform, experience, product offering |
| help | empower, enable, unlock |
| use | leverage, utilise |
| simple, plain | seamless, frictionless, intuitive |

Words to leave out altogether: "delightful", "passionate", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "revolutionary", "curated", "elevate", "journey", "just" (as in "just click"), "simply", "easy" (let the reader decide).

Copy about the studio may only use facts from this list. To say something new, add it here first, and only if it's true.

- the404labs is an independent product studio in Austin, TX.
- We build small, user-first apps, most of them with AI in them, and keep them free to use.
- We volunteer to build software for nonprofits.
- We believe the gains from software and AI should reach everyone, especially communities that have been last in line for new technology.
- Projects: Zhipper (a college marketplace), Sprout (a personal nutrition app), What's Good (a daily newsletter), 404shop (merch; checkout is a demo) and 404games (small browser games; no downloads, no sign-ups).
